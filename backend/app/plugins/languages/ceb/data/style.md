**Cebuano (Bisaya) Authenticity Rules — Read Carefully**

You are generating **Cebuano as spoken in Cebu and northern Mindanao** (Bisaya / Binisaya), in
standard modern spelling. Generate Cebuano only — NEVER Tagalog/Filipino, Hiligaynon, Waray,
Spanish sentences, or English. **Tagalog is the danger:** it is closely related, it dominates
your training data, and a Tagalog word in a Cebuano sentence looks plausible. Treat every
Tagalog form below as an error.

**Do not drift into Tagalog — the Tagalog form is on the LEFT, write the Cebuano on the right:**
- "hindi" → "dili" (no/not); "wala" stays "wala" (there is none)
- "ano" → "unsa" (what); "saan" → "asa" (where); "kailan" → "kanus-a" (when);
  "sino" → "kinsa" (who); "bakit" → "ngano" (why); "paano" → "unsaon" (how)
- "magkano" → "pila" (how much); "ilan" → "pila" (how many)
- "ngayon" → "karon" (now); "kahapon" → "gahapon"; "bukas" → "ugma"; "kanina" → "ganiha"
- "ito" → "kini"/"ni"; "iyan" → "kana"/"na"; "iyon" → "kadto"; "dito" → "dinhi";
  "doon" → "didto"
- "tayo" → "kita"/"ta" (we, inclusive); "kayo" → "kamo"/"mo" (you, plural)
- "kasi" → "kay" (because); "din"/"rin" → "pud"/"sad" (also)
- "maraming salamat" → "daghang salamat"; "salamat po" → "salamat"
- "umaga" → "buntag" (morning); "Magandang umaga" → "Maayong buntag"
- "kumain" → "mikaon"/"mokaon" (see verbs below)

**The Tagalog case marker "ng" does not exist in Cebuano.** Use:
- "sa" for possession and for the non-focus actor or a definite object:
  "ang balay sa akong lola" (my grandmother's house)
- "og" for an indefinite object: "Mopalit ko og isda." (I will buy fish)
- "ug" for "and": "isda ug kan-on" (fish and rice)
- For personal names: "si" (focus), "ni" (actor, non-focus), "kang" (location/recipient):
  "Gihatag ni Maria kang Juan."
- The Tagalog "ay" inversion ("Ako ay...") is NOT Cebuano. Never use "ay".

**No "po" or "opo". Cebuano has no politeness particle, and "po" is the single most
recognizable Tagalog marker.** Show respect instead through:
- address terms: "Nong" / "Nang" (to an older man / woman), "Lola" / "Lolo", "Ma'am" / "Sir"
  to an official or a shopkeeper;
- "palihug" (please): "Palihug, pila man ni?";
- a softer register: "Pasayloa ko" (forgive me/sorry), "Salamat kaayo".
Yes is "oo" to anyone; there is no "opo".

**Verbs MUST carry a mood/aspect affix in a statement.** A bare root with a pronoun is wrong:
"Palit ko og isda" is an error; write "Mopalit ko og isda" (I will buy fish) or
"Mipalit ko og isda" (I bought fish).
- Actor focus: "mo-" future/intended ("Mokaon ko"), "mi-" or "ni-" completed ("Mikaon ko",
  "Nikaon ko"), "nag-" ongoing or completed with "mag-" verbs ("Nagluto siya"), "mag-" future
  ("Magluto ko").
- Object focus: "-on" future ("Kaonon nako ang isda"), "gi-" completed ("Gikaon nako ang isda").
- Location/benefactive focus: "-an" future, "gi-...-an" completed ("Gitabangan nako siya").
- Commands may use the bare root or "palihug": "Lingkod, Nong." "Palihug hulat kadiyot."
- Pronoun order: the short actor pronoun follows the verb: "Mokaon ko", "Miadto sila".

**The linker is "nga"** between a modifier and what it modifies ("bugnaw nga tubig", "maayo nga
tawo"). After a vowel it is often contracted to "-ng" in set phrases: "Maayong buntag",
"Maayong gabii". Counting uses "ka": "usa ka tasa", "duha ka tiket".

**Numbers — use each system where Cebuano speakers use it. The learner must meet BOTH.**
- **Clock time → Spanish-derived** with "alas": "alas otso sa buntag" (8 a.m.), "alas dos y
  medya" (2:30), "alas singko sa hapon" (5 p.m.).
- **Prices → Spanish-derived** peso amounts: "baynte pesos", "singkwenta pesos",
  "siyento baynte".
- **Counting things and people → native** with "ka": "usa ka kape", "duha ka tiket",
  "tulo ka anak", "lima ka minuto".
- Native: usa, duha, tulo, upat, lima, unom, pito, walo, siyam, napulo.
- Spanish-derived: uno, dos, tres, kwatro, singko, sais, siyete, otso, nuwebe, dyis, onse,
  dose, baynte, traynta, kwarenta, singkwenta, sisenta, siyento.
Write prices and times in WORDS, never digits.

**False friends with Tagalog — the same word means something else. Use the CEBUANO meaning:**
- "pila" = how much / how many (Tagalog: a queue)
- "bukid" = mountain (Tagalog: a farm, a field)
- "langgam" = bird (Tagalog: an ant)
- "libog" = confused (Tagalog: lust). Avoid it in dialogue entirely; the Tagalog reading is
  vulgar, and "wala ko kasabot" (I don't understand) carries the meaning safely.

**No Bislish code-switching.** Do not switch into English phrases mid-sentence ("Wait lang",
"Sorry kaayo", "Actually, ..."). A single everyday word that Cebuano speakers use as their own
("okay", as in "Okay ra ko") is acceptable; English clauses are not. Everyday loans in nativized spelling ARE correct:
- From Spanish: "oras" (time), "mesa", "silya", "kutsara", "bintana", "kwarto", "sapatos",
  "gusto" (want/like), "pero" (but), "siguro" (maybe), "sige" (okay)
- From English: "traysikel", "dyip", "bus", "taksi", "kape"
Never reach for a rare purist coinage to avoid a loan.

**Natural everyday phrases:**
- "Maayong buntag/hapon/gabii", "Kumusta ka?" / "Kumusta man ka?"
- "Maayo man, salamat." (I'm fine, thanks)
- "Salamat" / "Daghang salamat" / "Salamat kaayo", "Walay sapayan" (you're welcome)
- "Pasayloa ko" (sorry), "Palihug" (please), "Kadiyot lang" (just a moment)
- "Pila man ni?" (how much is this?), "Asa ka padulong?" (where are you going?)
- "Sige" (okay / go ahead), "Amping!" (take care), "Adios" / "Babay" (goodbye)

**Quality examples:**
CORRECT: "Maayong hapon, Nang. Pila man ning isda?" (respect by address term, no po)
CORRECT: "Mopalit ko og duha ka kilo nga bugas." (mo- verb, og object, ka counter)
CORRECT: "Alas otso na. Mokaon na ta!" (Spanish clock time, inclusive "ta")
CORRECT: "Didto ko sa balay sa akong lola gahapon." ("sa" possession, "gahapon")
WRONG: "Magkano po ito?" (Tagalog — say "Pila man ni?"), "Hindi ko alam." (Tagalog — say "Dili ko kahibalo.")
WRONG: "Palit ko og isda." (bare root — say "Mopalit ko og isda.")
WRONG: "Kumain ako ng isda." (Tagalog verb and "ng" — say "Mikaon ko og isda.")
WRONG: "Ako ay taga-Cebu." (Tagalog "ay" inversion — say "Taga-Sugbo ko.")

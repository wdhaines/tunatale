**Tagalog Authenticity Rules — Read Carefully**

You are generating **Tagalog as spoken in Metro Manila**, in standard modern spelling. Generate
Tagalog only — NEVER Cebuano/Bisaya, Ilocano, Spanish sentences, or Malay/Indonesian.

**Do not drift into Cebuano or Malay** (the closest confusables):
- "ano" (what), NEVER Cebuano "unsa"; "saan" (where), NEVER "asa"; "hindi" (no/not), NEVER "dili"
- "ngayon" (now), NEVER "karon"; "maraming salamat", NEVER "salamat kaayo"
- NEVER Malay "terima kasih", "tidak", "apa kabar"

**NO Taglish code-switching.** Do not switch into English phrases or clauses mid-sentence
("Wait lang", "Actually, ...", "Sorry ha", "So ano na?"), and never use English function words.
The English fillers models slip in most — NEVER use them; the Tagalog is on the right:
"Hi"/"Hey" → "Uy"/"Kumusta"; "okay" → "sige"/"ayos"; "Perfect!"/"Nice!" → "Ayos!"/"Galing!";
"See you" → "Kita tayo mamaya"; "shopping" → "namimili"; "I'm late" → "Nahuli ako";
"Kita kits" is slang, avoid it. Where the English word has become the everyday Tagalog word,
use its nativized spelling instead of English: "trapik" (traffic), "miting" (meeting),
"mag-text" (to text). NEVER swap in a formal purist word ("pulong") for these.
Write prices and times in WORDS, never digits ("bente pesos", not "P20").
**BUT loanwords that simply ARE the everyday Tagalog word are correct — use them.** Write them in
their nativized spelling:
- From Spanish: "oras" (time/hour), "kotse" (car), "mesa" (table), "silya" (chair), "kutsara"
  (spoon), "bintana" (window), "pera" (money), "trabaho" (work), "kuwarto" (room), "sapatos"
  (shoes), "gusto" (want/like), "pero" (but), "siguro" (maybe), "sige" (okay/go ahead)
- From English: "restawran", "kompyuter", "telebisyon", "bus", "taksi", "diyaryo", "kape"
- **NEVER reach for a rare purist coinage to avoid a loan.** The coinage is the error:
  "eroplano", NEVER "salipawpaw"; "silya" or "upuan", NEVER "salumpuwit"; "matematika", NEVER "sipnayan".
  Rule of thumb: write what a Manila speaker actually calls the thing, minus any English phrase.

**Politeness: "po"/"opo" the way a courteous adult visitor uses them — NOT the constant register
of a hotel or restaurant worker.**
- Use "po" when the SPEAKER addresses someone older, a stranger, a shopkeeper or an official:
  "Magkano po ito?" "Salamat po." "Magandang umaga po." With them, answer "opo" (yes) and address
  them with plural "kayo"/"nila", not "ikaw"/"mo": "Kumusta po kayo?"
- **At most ONE "po" per sentence**, placed after the first word or phrase. Never stack it
  ("Opo, ito po ay po..."), and do not put it in every clause.
- **No "po" between friends, family of the same generation, or when speaking to children** — and
  that holds even when repeating a phrase used with the vendor earlier ("Pabili po" is for the
  vendor; to a friend it is "Bumili ako ng prutas"). Friends say "oo" (yes) and "ikaw"/"ka".
- A waiter or shop assistant character may use "po" to a customer, but at the same moderate rate:
  one per turn, not one per phrase. Avoid the "ho"/"oho" variant; use "po"/"opo".

**Numbers — use each system in the context Filipinos actually use it. The learner must meet BOTH.**
- **Clock time → Spanish-derived**, with "alas-": "alas-otso ng umaga" (8 a.m.), "alas-dos y
  medya" (2:30), "alas-una ng hapon" (1 p.m.).
- **Prices → Spanish-derived** for peso amounts: "bente pesos", "singkuwenta pesos", "siyento
  beinte". Small change may be native: "sampung piso".
- **Counting things and people → native**, with the linker: "isang kape" (one coffee), "dalawang
  tiket", "tatlong anak", "limang minuto".
- Ages: native for small children ("limang taon"); Spanish-derived is natural for adults
  ("bente singko anyos").
- Native: isa, dalawa, tatlo, apat, lima, anim, pito, walo, siyam, sampu, labing-isa,
  dalawampu, isandaan. Spanish-derived: uno, dos, tres, kuwatro, singko, sais, siyete, otso,
  nuwebe, diyes, onse, dose, bente, trenta, singkuwenta, siyento.

**Markers are the core of Tagalog grammar — get them right:**
- "ang" marks the topic/focus; "ng" (pronounced "nang") marks the non-focus actor or object;
  "sa" marks location, direction, recipient, or a definite object.
- For personal names: "si" (focus), "ni" (non-focus), "kay" (location/recipient). Plurals:
  "sina", "nina", "kina".
- "mga" (pronounced "manga") marks plural: "mga bata". NEVER pluralize with an English "-s".

**"ng" vs "nang" — a very common written error:**
- "ng" = the marker: "Kumain ako ng mangga." / "bahay ng kaibigan ko"
- "nang" = "when"/"so that", manner, and between repeated verbs: "Umuwi siya nang maaga."
  "Tawa nang tawa ang bata." "Nang dumating siya, kumain kami."

**The linker "na"/"-ng" joins a modifier to what it modifies:**
- after a vowel, attach "-ng": "magandang umaga", "isang tasa"
- after "n", attach "-g": "kaibigang matalik"
- otherwise write "na" as a separate word: "mabait na bata", "malamig na tubig"

**Word order is predicate-first.** The natural spoken order puts the verb or predicate first:
"Kumain ako ng kanin." "Maganda ang panahon ngayon."
- The "ay" inversion ("Ako ay kumain") is formal and written. In dialogue, use it RARELY.
- Short pronouns and particles cluster right after the first word: "Kumain ka na ba?" "Hindi pa
  ako kumakain." "Pupunta rin kami."

**Affix spelling — models get these wrong constantly:**
- "mag-"/"nag-" take a hyphen ONLY before a vowel: "mag-aral", "nag-ayos". Before a consonant
  they join directly: "magkape", "nagluto", "namimili" — NEVER "mag-kape", "nag-luto", "nag-shopping".
- Use the standard affix for each verb; do not invent one. "bili" (buy) is an -um- verb:
  "bumili" (bought), "bumibili" (is buying), "bibili" (will buy) — NEVER "nagbili", "nag-bili",
  "kumabili". Likewise "kumain", "pumunta", "uminom", "umuwi".
- "Kumusta", not "kamusta" (standard spelling).

**Verbs mark ASPECT, not tense.** Completed "kumain" (ate), in progress "kumakain" (is eating),
contemplated "kakain" (will eat). Choose the aspect the scene needs; vary focus naturally
("Kumain ako ng isda" actor focus vs "Kinain ko ang isda" object focus).

**Natural everyday phrases:**
- "Magandang umaga/hapon/gabi (po)", "Kumusta (po) kayo?" / "Kumusta ka?" (to a friend)
- "Mabuti naman, salamat." (I'm fine, thanks — NOT the literal "Ako ay mabuti")
- "Salamat (po)" / "Maraming salamat (po)", "Walang anuman" (you're welcome)
- "Pasensya na (po)" (sorry), "Makikiraan po" (excuse me, coming through)
- "Pabili po ng ..." (I'd like to buy ...), "Magkano po?" (how much?)
- "Sige" (okay / go ahead), "Ingat!" (take care), "Paalam" (goodbye; "Sige, alis na ako" is casual)

**Quality examples:**
CORRECT: "Magandang hapon po. Pabili po ng dalawang pandesal." (customer to an older vendor: one po per sentence)
CORRECT: "Alas-otso na. Tara, kain na tayo!" (between friends: no po, Spanish clock time)
CORRECT: "Magkano po ito?" "Bente pesos po." (price in Spanish-derived numbers)
CORRECT: "Sumakay kami ng bus papunta sa opisina." (predicate-first, everyday loanword "bus")
WRONG: "Wait lang, check ko muna." (Taglish), "Unsa ni?" (Cebuano), "Ako ay mabuti." (stilted calque)
WRONG: "Opo po, salamat po, sige po." (po stacked in every phrase), "Kumain ako nang kanin." (nang for ng)
WRONG: "Nag-bili lang ako." (wrong affix + wrong hyphen — say "Bumili lang ako."), "Perfect, see you!" (English)
WRONG: "Sumakay kami ng salipawpaw." (purist coinage — say "eroplano")
